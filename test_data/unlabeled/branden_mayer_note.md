Chief complaint: Routine follow-up for hypertension, ischemic heart disease, and
prediabetes.

History of present illness: Patient is a 101-year-old man with a history of essential
hypertension, ischemic heart disease with prior coronary artery bypass grafting,
prediabetes, obesity, and anemia, presenting for a scheduled follow-up visit. He denies
chest pain, shortness of breath, or palpitations today. He does report intermittent
lightheadedness when standing up from a seated position over the past month; he has not
fallen but has started using a cane for balance when walking outside the home. He also
reports that about a year ago he broke out in hives with lip swelling after taking a
sulfa-based antibiotic prescribed for a urinary tract infection; he does not recall the
exact drug name but has avoided sulfa drugs since.

Vital signs: Blood pressure 162/94 mmHg (seated), heart rate 88 bpm, temperature 97.9 F,
respiratory rate 18. Orthostatic blood pressure not formally measured today.

Assessment: Blood pressure is elevated and above goal despite current regimen. History
of an adverse drug reaction consistent with a sulfa antibiotic allergy. Reported
lightheadedness on standing raises concern for orthostatic hypotension, notable given
his antihypertensive regimen and history of ischemic heart disease.

Plan: Document sulfa drug allergy in the chart. Recheck blood pressure and obtain formal
orthostatic vital signs at next visit. Continue current cardiac medications; reassess
antihypertensive dosing if orthostatic symptoms persist.
