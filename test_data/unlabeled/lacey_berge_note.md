Chief complaint: Follow-up for chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes, and asthma.

History of present illness: Patient is a 56-year-old woman with known chronic kidney
disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus (on insulin), and asthma (on inhaled fluticasone and
albuterol), presenting for a routine follow-up visit. She reports her breathing has been
generally stable but notes occasional shortness of breath with exertion, using her
albuterol rescue inhaler about twice a week. She also reports a new itchy rash on both
forearms that started about two weeks ago, shortly after her primary care physician
briefly started her on lisinopril for blood pressure; the lisinopril was discontinued
and the rash has since improved. No fever, chest pain, or swelling of the face or throat.

Vital signs: Blood pressure 148/92 mmHg, heart rate 78 bpm, temperature 98.2 F,
respiratory rate 16, SpO2 97% on room air.

Assessment: Blood pressure is elevated today. New adverse skin reaction temporally
associated with lisinopril, consistent with a drug allergy; agent already discontinued.
Asthma symptoms are mild and only partially controlled, reflected by twice-weekly
rescue inhaler use.

Plan: Document lisinopril allergy in the chart. Continue current diabetes and asthma
regimen. Recheck blood pressure at next visit; consider alternative antihypertensive
given the lisinopril reaction. Continue hydrochlorothiazide.
